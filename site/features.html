Features

Five data layers, one timeline.

Each layer is real, current, and free to use. The script decides when each one appears — you do not keyframe anything.

01 — Terrain

Real elevation, lit properly.

Global terrain at up to 15 zoom levels, with shaded relief whose sun is anchored to the map — so shadows stay put as the camera orbits instead of swinging around with it.

The camera height is smoothed against the terrain profile, so it rises over a mountain range without lurching at every ridge.

02 — Satellite

Any day since 2000.

NASA GIBS true-colour imagery is time-enabled: name a date and you get that day's pass. Name two and the film cross-fades between them — before and after a flood, a fire, a season.

Nine curated layers including night lights, cloud-top temperature, snow cover, aerosols and active fire detections.

03 — Population

Where people actually are.

Kontur's H3 hexagon grid, draped over the terrain and coloured by density. The scale is derived from the cells in view, not a fixed constant, so a country does not flatten into one colour.

Resolution is matched to the shot automatically: 22 km cells for a continent, 3 km for a country, 400 m for a city.

04 — Borders & weather

Lines that mean something.

International boundaries drawn as a glowing line that arrives when the script names them. Weather layers — storms, snow, smoke, fires — as a timed raster over the same ground.

Under the hood

Engineering you only notice when it is missing.

◎

Deterministic frames

Not a screen recording. Each frame sets the camera, waits until the map is genuinely settled, then captures. Two runs give identical pixels.

⇣

Exact prefetch

The camera model is verified against the browser's own projection, so the engine knows precisely which tiles each frame needs and fetches them first.

⏱

Word-level sync

Narration is force-aligned word by word, so an overlay can land on a specific phrase rather than a rough timestamp.

🎚

Broadcast-shaped audio

Music is loudness-normalised then ducked under the voice by sidechain compression, so any track you drop in sits at the same level.

⚡

GPU encode

Frames go out through the hardware h264 encoder at constant quality, tagged limited-range BT.709 so players do not shift the grade.

⛓

Offline render loop

Once tiles are cached the frame loop makes zero network requests — verifiable with a strict mode that refuses to fetch mid-render.
